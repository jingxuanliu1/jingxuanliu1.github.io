01 / Problem and motivation

Fatigue is temporal; a single eye prediction is not the system.

A classifier can label a cropped eye as open or closed, but a driver-fatigue pipeline also has to find the face and eyes in imperfect frames, preserve left/right evidence, handle missing detections, aggregate behavior through time, and produce outputs that a researcher can inspect.

Research question: how can eye-state predictions from recorded video and live camera feeds be transformed into a PERCLOS timeline without hiding missing detections or overstating classifier evidence?

02 / Project scope

From labeled eye crops to annotated temporal output.

The pipeline detects eye regions, prepares 64-by-32 grayscale crops, runs a MobileNetV3Small-based open/closed classifier, applies temporal and agreement rules, and aggregates valid detections into a sliding-window PERCLOS timeline.

  • Recorded-video analysis and live-camera analysis.
  • dlib 81-point facial-landmark eye cropping.
  • Separate left-eye and right-eye probabilities.
  • Hybrid decisions using classifier probability, eye-aspect ratio, dark-center evidence, and left/right consistency.
  • Annotated frames, CSV outputs, and a temporal PERCLOS graph.
  • Public-safe audit tooling that emits counts and hashes rather than copying restricted images.

03 / My contribution

I connected model development to research integrity and end-to-end behavior.

I developed the MobileNetV3Small-based eye-state classifier, audited the labeled raw tree for exact duplicates and split-leakage risks, and integrated recorded-video and live-camera predictions into annotated PERCLOS timelines.

The public-safe case study and tooling preserve the audit and aggregation logic without redistributing participant media, face images, restricted datasets, or the large private research package.

04 / Architecture

Detection quality and classifier quality stay separate.

05 / Important engineering decisions

Research integrity shaped the pipeline.

Audit content hashes before trusting a random split

The raw tree contains 47,236 files but only 32,932 unique SHA-256 hashes; 14,304 instances beyond the first copy are exact duplicates.

Keep missing detection explicit

Frames without reliable face or eye detection are invalid, not forced into an open or closed label that would bias PERCLOS.

Preserve left and right evidence

Separate eye predictions and agreement rules make it possible to inspect asymmetry and low-confidence cases.

Separate classifier evaluation from the full pipeline

A model can classify pre-cropped eyes while the end-to-end system still fails under pose, lighting, glasses, motion blur, or small-eye conditions.

Publish code and aggregates, not restricted media

The public-safe boundary excludes participant videos, face crops, raw restricted datasets, and private checkpoints.

06 / Technical challenges

The hardest errors occur between model and environment.

  • Duplicate leakage: exact duplicates can appear convincing in an image-level split while weakening independence.
  • Incomplete subject identity: only 6,000 filenames expose recognized subject tokens; 41,236 files do not support a reliable subject-independent split.
  • Detection gaps: pose, lighting, reflections, blur, and small eyes produce intervals without usable crops.
  • Temporal aggregation: PERCLOS must operate on valid detected-eye frames while preserving coverage information.
  • Privacy: debugging and demonstration artifacts must not expose participant media or restricted data.

09 / Limitations

The missing training lineage limits the conclusion.

  • No immutable manifest ties the candidate checkpoint to exact training, validation, and test file hashes.
  • Most files lack participant identity, preventing a complete subject-independent split audit.
  • The diagnostic subset was not reserved by a recorded pre-training protocol.
  • No manual video ground truth is paired with the selected full-duration output.
  • Several recordings contain substantial missing-detection intervals; classifier behavior and end-to-end reliability must remain separate.
  • The public repository excludes raw data, participant recordings, restricted face crops, and the private research package.

10 / What I learned

A trustworthy ML result begins before model training.

This research changed how I evaluate machine-learning systems. Dataset identity, duplicate structure, split provenance, detector coverage, and missing-output semantics can matter as much as architecture choice.

The next iteration needs a hash-locked training manifest, a pre-registered subject/source split, matched video ground truth, detector-coverage reporting, and end-to-end timing that includes every stage—not just classifier calls.

11 / Repository and evidence

The public boundary is inspectable and privacy-aware.

The public repository contains the machine-learning and PERCLOS pipeline code, setup and execution instructions, dataset references, and the public-safe project structure. The deeper audit is supported by saved aggregate JSON and claim-verification reports; restricted media is not redistributed.
